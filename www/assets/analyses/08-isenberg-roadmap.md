## 1. Core thesis

Greg's point is narrower than "be a better CRM": people avoid tiny loops because
the pain is **context + a decision + an uncomfortable send**. The product is an
agent that does the first 90% so the user only hits send. The go-to-market rule
is **one loop, nailed**.

LoopKeeper already chose that loop: *"I should really reach out."* Everything
below is that idea turned into features users can feel inside
`zyppar.com/loopkeeper`.

---

## 2. The wedge — build these first (features 1–10)

1. **Open Loops inbox** — Not a contact book. A list of unfinished human loops:
   owed replies, promised intros, "should check in," "said I'd send this."
   One row = one closeable thing.
2. **90% agent, 10% you** — For each loop the agent finds context, picks a next
   action, drafts the message. You approve and send. If the user still has to
   hunt through notes, you missed the tweet.
3. **Context packet** — On every loop: who, how you know them, last touch, open
   promise, why this is sitting, suggested stance (warm / brief /
   overdue-apology). The "digging up context" step, productized.
4. **Decision chip** — Three buttons only: *Send draft*, *Wait until [date]*,
   *Drop and archive*. Avoided loops survive because people will not decide.
   Force the decision.
5. **Uncomfortable-message drafter** — The hard ones: late reply, awkward ask,
   declined invite, money, "we fell off." Tone sliders: short, honest, light,
   formal. Default to short. Long drafts are how loops stay open.
6. **One-tap send** — Gmail / iMessage / WhatsApp / LinkedIn where you already
   talk. Draft lives in LoopKeeper; send happens in the real channel. No new
   inbox to check.
7. **Nudge until send** — Rising reminder on the same loop, then silence after
   close. Keep it as the habit engine, not a second product.
8. **Closed-loop receipt** — After send: log what went out, when, and what
   "done" means (reply needed vs loop actually closed). "Sent" is not always
   "closed."
9. **Today's 3** — Hard cap. Isenberg's 47 is the enemy. If the inbox shows 47,
   people will avoid the app the same way they avoid the tasks.
10. **Capture in one sentence** — "Need to reply to Priya about the Thursday
    slot." Agent turns that into a loop with person, deadline, draft. The old
    RolodexAI natural-language intake, pointed at loops instead of contact
    cards.

## 3. Features that make the 90% actually work (11–20)

11. **Owed-reply detector** — People you have not answered. Highest-guilt
    loops. If you can close only one class first, close this class.
12. **Promise extractor** — From your own notes and past messages: "I'll intro
    you," "I'll send the deck," "I'll ping after the trip." Promises are loops
    with a name on them.
13. **Staleness reason** — "Sitting 16 days because the reply requires a
    decision about pricing." Name the friction. Users avoid what they cannot
    name.
14. **Pretext generator** — A reach-out with no hook gets skipped. Suggest a
    real hook: last topic, their post, a date, a mutual, an apology for the
    gap. No fake "just circling back" unless that is all that is left.
15. **Channel picker** — Same person, different discomfort: email vs text vs
    voice note. Agent recommends the lowest-friction channel for this loop,
    not a default.
16. **Voice-note option** — For loops that feel stiff as text. Agent outlines
    20 seconds of what to say. User records. Many avoided replies are tone
    problems, not content problems.
17. **Waiting-on-them vs waiting-on-you** — Split the inbox. You can only close
    your side. The other pile should not generate guilt nudges.
18. **Snooze with a condition** — "After their launch." "After I land." "If
    they have not replied by Friday, escalate." Blind snooze is how loops rot.
19. **Drop with dignity** — Archive plus a one-line reason: wrong time, not
    worth it, relationship cooled. Closing by dropping is still closing. The
    tweet is about finishing, not about becoming more social.
20. **Loop templates for the three most common human avoids** — Late reply.
    Check-in after silence. Ask for a favor. Nail those three drafts before you
    invent a general agent.

## 4. Features that keep LoopKeeper a people product (21–26)

Do these **only after the wedge works**. They are still on-brand because they
are loops attached to a person.

21. **Intro loop** — You promised to connect A and B. Agent drafts both notes,
    waits for your send, tracks whether the intro happened.
22. **Thank-you / condolence / congrats loop** — Time-sensitive social debts.
    These die in 48 hours. Fast draft or they are worthless.
23. **Meeting-follow-up loop** — Auto-open after a calendar event with a person
    in the book: recap + promised next step + draft.
24. **Birthday / work-anniversary only when you actually care** — Opt-in per
    person. Generic annual spam is the old rolodex. LoopKeeper should only
    surface the ones you would be embarrassed to miss.
25. **"We should grab coffee" debt** — The most common fake loop. Agent asks:
    schedule it, send a real invite, or drop it. No more eternal coffee.
26. **Small-favor tracker** — Borrowed book, open ask, outstanding invoice
    between friends. Still a human loop. Still context + uncomfortable
    message.

## 5. Adjacent loops — park unless you want a different company (27–30)

Isenberg's examples are real businesses. Most of them should **not** live in
LoopKeeper unless you explicitly expand the job from "people I owe" to "all
avoided admin."

27. **Warranty / rebate claims** — money loop, form-filling agent. Separate
    wedge.
28. **"I should switch" plans** — insurance, phone, power. Comparison agent.
    Separate wedge.
29. **Kid-logistics** — RSVP, permission slip, camp deadline. Parent wedge.
    Separate wedge.
30. **Dispute-a-charge / reschedule-the-appointment** — classic avoided admin.
    Only pull these in if the same user already trusts you on people-loops and
    asks for "the rest of my 47."

If you add 27–30 now, the name still works and the product becomes mush. The
tweet's other line matters as much as the vision: **the wedge is one loop
first.**

## 6. Two product features about the product itself (31–32)

31. **First-session win = one send** — Onboarding is not "import LinkedIn."
    It is: pick one sitting loop, generate the draft, hit send. That is PMF
    for this idea.
32. **Show the 47 shrinking** — A single number: open loops this week vs last
    week. Users will forgive a crude UI if that number falls. They will not
    forgive a beautiful contact database that grows while nothing closes.

## 7. How many, in practice

- 32 ideas.
- **10 to build.**
- **1 to bet the rebrand on.**

The bet: **LoopKeeper is the agent for avoided human messages**, not a second
brain for every chore in the tweet.

Greg's three examples (warranties, plan switching, school forms) are proof that
the pattern is huge — they are not a feature roadmap for a product whose
current use is "nudge me until I reach out." Stay on that loop until drafts get
sent without the user reopening the mental tab. Then add intro loops and
meeting follow-ups. Only then consider admin loops, and only as a different
mode or a different app under Zyppar.

---

## 8. Build rule (the corrected takeaway)

> context packet → decision → draft → send → closed receipt

Anything that does not make that path shorter is decoration.
