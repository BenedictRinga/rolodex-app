You owe someone a reply.
Everybody does.

LoopKeeper is a small Android app that catches the replies, promises and follow-ups you keep meaning to send. You type one line — "call Baba about the land papers" — it drafts the message, you hit Send, and the loop is closed.

What I'm asking (it's small)

15 minutes a day, for two weeks. Feed it one real reply you owe, let it draft, press Send. That's the whole habit.

Press Send and you're done. The loop closes the moment you send — you never sit waiting on anyone. If they reply, the app surfaces it as a fresh note; nothing hangs on you.

When something annoys you, say it in the app's chat. Honest feedback is the real job.

Why this matters more than it looks

Psychology has a name for the tax you pay on every unsent reply: the Zeigarnik effect — unfinished tasks don't leave quietly, they hold a tab open in your mind, humming in the background.

Close the loops and the tab shuts: calmer mind, warmer relationships (people feel remembered, not managed), cleaner business (opportunities don't die in the "I keep meaning to"). LoopKeeper isn't a contacts manager — it's how you stop renting head-space to what you haven't said.
